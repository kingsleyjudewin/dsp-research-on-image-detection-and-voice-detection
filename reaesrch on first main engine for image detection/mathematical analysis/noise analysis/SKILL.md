# Noise-Pattern Forgery Detection

## Purpose

Every imaging sensor imprints a unique, signal-dependent noise fingerprint onto every photo it captures: fixed-pattern photo-response non-uniformity (PRNU) from pixel-to-pixel sensitivity variations, plus random shot/read noise whose local variance depends on scene brightness, ISO, and the camera's in-pipeline denoising. When a region from a different image (different sensor, different capture settings, or a CGI/GAN patch with no sensor noise at all) is spliced into a host image, or when a region is smoothed/copy-moved/inpainted, the noise statistics of that region stop matching its surroundings. Because noise is a low-level, content-independent signal (unlike edges, lighting, or JPEG grids, which forgers can visually retouch to look "right"), inconsistency in noise level or noise correlation across regions is a strong, semantically-orthogonal tampering cue that survives many forms of visual retouching and is well suited to fusion with other engines (ELA, CFA, copy-move, splicing-transformer) in a multi-cue forgery-detection pipeline.

## Techniques found in the literature

**1. Determining Image Origin and Integrity Using Sensor Noise** (Chen, Fridrich, Goljan, Lukáš, IEEE TIFS 2008) — `chen2008.pdf`
The foundational PRNU forensics paper. Models sensor output as `I = I⁽⁰⁾ + I⁽⁰⁾K + Θ`, where `K` is the multiplicative PRNU factor. `K` is estimated per-camera via a maximum-likelihood estimator from N images: `K̂ = Σ(Wₖ·Iₖ)/Σ(Iₖ)²` where `Wₖ = Iₖ − F(Iₖ)` is the denoised noise residual (Mihcak wavelet-based denoising filter). For integrity verification, a sliding 128×128 block is tested via a Neyman–Pearson hypothesis test on the normalized correlation `ρ = corr(Xᵦ, Wᵦ)` between the block's noise residual and `I·K̂`; blocks lacking correlation with the camera's PRNU are flagged tampered. A trained correlation predictor (using image intensity, texture, and "signal flattening" features) estimates the expected `ρ` per block to set adaptive thresholds. Reported: on 345 Canon G2 forgeries at JPEG-90, 85% of forgeries had ≥2/3 of the tampered region correctly localized; degrades on heavily textured/dark/saturated blocks and needs the source camera's PRNU (reference-based). Robust to JPEG, gamma correction, resizing, moderate denoising.

**2. PRNU-based Detection of Morphed Face Images** (Debiasi, Scherhag, Rathgeb, Uhl, Busch, 2018) — `debiasi2018.pdf`
Blind (no reference camera needed), region-based use of PRNU spectral statistics for face-morph detection, directly transferable to general splicing detection. Splits the extracted PRNU into N equisized cells (up to 10×10), computes the DFT magnitude spectrum of each cell's PRNU, and derives per-cell scalar features from the DFT magnitude histogram: peak value `P_val = max H(n)`, peak position `P_pos = argmax H(n)`, and combined `P_pv = P_val · P_pos`. Cell scores are aggregated globally via mean or RMS. Best result: D-EER as low as 1.4–2.2% with 8×8 cell fragmentation; robust to scaling/sharpening but defeated by histogram equalization. Demonstrates that splitting PRNU into local cells (rather than one global correlation) is what exposes localized tampering — directly useful design pattern even without face-morphing context.

**3. Gaussian and Impulse Noise Identification from Image Using Frequency Domain Analysis** (Jain, Arolkar, 2024) — `Gaussian-and-Impulse-Noise-Identification...pdf`
Not a forgery-detection paper per se — a noise-*type* classifier — but directly useful as a pre-processing/characterization module. Computes the FFT magnitude spectrum of an image, then the mean magnitude and standard deviation of that spectrum. Classifies noise type via a z-score: `z = (mean_magnitude − avg_mean_magnitude_train) / std_dev`; z < −1 → Gaussian blur/noise, z > −1 → impulse (salt-and-pepper) noise, else "no noise" (threshold band [−1,1]). Rationale: Gaussian noise/blur suppresses high-frequency spectral content (lower mean magnitude), impulse noise adds spectral outlier spikes (raises it). Reported accuracy: 81.25% Gaussian, 55.56% impulse, 60% no-noise on Kadid10k-derived test set — thresholds are dataset-specific and the method is simple/heuristic, so treat as a coarse global noise-type gate rather than a localization tool.

**4. Determining PRNU refinements — general context (chen2008, see above) and Korus & Huang, "Multi-scale analysis strategies in PRNU-based tampering localization"** (cited in book bibliography, not separately downloaded): extends single-scale PRNU correlation to a multi-scale fusion, reducing block-size bias — relevant if implementing PRNU localization, since block size trades off spatial resolution against reliable correlation statistics.

**Marginally relevant papers** (included per instructions, noise-detection relevance is secondary/absent):

- **Fusing Multi-scale Attention and Transformer for Detection and Localization of Image Splicing Forgery** (Xu, Zheng, Shao, 2024) — `Fusing-Multi-scale-Attention...pdf`. A deep CNN+Transformer (CSIA cross-scale attention + DTransformer) pixel-localization network for splicing forgery, trained end-to-end on CASIA/COLUMB/NIST'16/Defacto. Not noise-based (no PRNU/noise-residual features) — it fuses multi-level CNN features with global self-attention context. Reported F=0.857/0.895/0.832 on the three datasets, outperforming DCT, CFA, ManTra-Net, AttU-Net baselines. Relevant only as a benchmark for what a learned end-to-end splicing localizer can achieve, and as a potential architecture template if a learned noise-residual branch is added later (analogous to how Noiseprint/RRU-Net use noise residuals as a CNN input channel).
- **Keypoint Based Tampered Image Identification** (Rajput, Dabhole, Prashantha, 2024) — `Keypoint-Based-Tampered-Image-Identification.pdf`. AKAZE keypoint extraction + brute-force matching + BIRCH clustering for copy-move forgery detection (96% accuracy on MICC-F220). Entirely geometric/keypoint-based, no noise analysis — belongs to the copy-move detection module category, not noise analysis. Included here only because it was in this folder; no noise-technique content to extract.
- **Policy Gradient-Driven Noise Mask** (Yavuz, Yang, 2025) — `Policy-Gradient-Driven-Noise-Mask.pdf`. Uses synthetic noise injection (Beta-distribution-sampled masks via RL/policy-gradient) purely as a *training-time regularizer* for medical-image classifiers, to improve cross-modality generalization. Noise here is added to images to help a classifier train, not analyzed as a forensic signal — essentially unrelated to forgery detection. No transferable technique for this engine beyond the general observation that noise statistics differ significantly across imaging domains/modalities (reinforces the "noise is scene/device dependent" premise).
- **Digital Image Forgery Detection: Techniques, Challenges and Applications** (Tyagi, Springer 2026) — `Digital-Image-Forgery-Detection.pdf`. A broad survey/textbook covering the whole forgery-detection field (active vs. passive, pixel/format/camera/geometry-based, deep learning taxonomy, datasets, evaluation metrics, challenges). Chapter 3.4.1.3 ("Camera-Based Image Forgery Detection") explicitly names "sensor noise patterns" alongside CFA interpolation and JPEG signatures as passive camera-fingerprint cues, and Chapter 2 flags that "the noise levels of source images used in splicing are different" as the core assumption exploited by splicing detectors. Its bibliography (entries such as Liu & Pun "Locating splicing forgery by noise estimation and vicinity noise descriptor", Zeng et al. "Image splicing localization using PCA-based noise level estimation", Meena & Tyagi "Image splicing forgery detection using noise level estimation", Cozzolino & Verdoliva "Noiseprint: a CNN-based camera model fingerprint", Korus & Huang "Multi-scale analysis strategies in PRNU-based tampering localization") is a useful pointer list for follow-up reading but the book itself gives no formulas/implementation detail — treat as background/context only, not a source of a specific technique.

## Recommended approach for this engine

1. **Primary detector — local noise-level inconsistency map.** Compute a per-block noise-level estimate across the image using one of: (a) local variance of the wavelet HH (or LH/HL) subband from a single-level DWT (fast, robust to texture if variance is measured on high-pass sub-bands rather than raw pixels — this is essentially what the Mihcak/Fridrich denoising filter in `chen2008.pdf` already does internally), or (b) PCA/robust-statistics based noise level estimation on flat/low-texture patches (per the noise-level-estimation line of work referenced in the Tyagi survey). Tile the image into overlapping blocks (128×128 as in Chen et al., or adaptive per resolution as in Debiasi et al.'s 8×8 cell study), compute a scalar noise-level per block, and flag blocks whose estimate deviates statistically from the image-wide (or locally-smoothed neighborhood) noise level.

2. **Secondary/confirmatory detector — PRNU correlation.** When a reference camera or a same-image self-consistency check is feasible, extract the PRNU noise residual `W = I − F(I)` with a wavelet denoising filter (Mihcak et al.), and compute block-wise normalized correlation `ρᵦ = corr(Xᵦ, Wᵦ)` against the expected camera PRNU (Chen et al.'s ML estimator) or, blind-mode, split the whole-image PRNU into cells and examine the DFT-magnitude-histogram peak features `P_val`/`P_pos`/`P_pv` per cell (Debiasi et al.) for internal inconsistency — no reference camera needed. Aggregate cell scores via mean or RMS. This is the higher-precision, higher-cost check; run it either on the whole image or only on regions the primary detector already flagged, to keep runtime bounded.

3. **Noise-type gate (cheap, optional).** Run a coarse FFT-spectrum-statistics classifier (mean/std of magnitude spectrum, z-score threshold, per Jain & Arolkar) to detect gross Gaussian-blur or impulse-noise anomalies (e.g., a region that was heavily denoised/smoothed to hide tampering, or has salt-and-pepper artifacts from re-compression). This is a fast triage signal, not a localization tool — use it to flag "this image/region has been globally or locally denoised" as a supplementary tampering indicator, since heavy local smoothing is itself the classic PRNU-defeat attack.

4. **Turning it into a score/heatmap for fusion.** For each block/cell, output a scalar anomaly score (e.g., z-scored deviation of local noise variance from the image's robust median noise level, or `1 − ρᵦ` for PRNU correlation, or the reciprocal of `P_val`/`P_pos` shift from the majority behavior). Normalize block scores to [0,1], upsample/interpolate to full image resolution (bilinear or same block-replication used by the block scanner) to produce a tampering heatmap, and also emit a single global scalar (max or top-k% mean of the block scores) for the fusion layer's decision-level combination with the other detection engines (copy-move/keypoint, splicing-transformer, ELA/CFA/format-based). Connected-component filtering (drop blobs below a minimum pixel count, as Chen et al. do at 64×64 px) and morphological dilation reduce false positives from block-boundary artifacts.

5. **Known limitations to document/handle:**
   - **Denoising/smoothing defeats it.** An attacker who denoises the spliced region (or the whole image) after tampering removes the very signal being measured — this is the single biggest failure mode reported across the PRNU literature and confirmed in Chen et al.'s Wiener-filter robustness tests (FRR degrades markedly).
   - **Needs sufficiently textured or at least non-saturated, non-flat regions.** PRNU is attenuated in saturated pixels (I≈255) and estimation is unreliable in extremely flat/dark regions; Chen et al.'s false positives cluster in saturated backgrounds with complex texture (e.g., tree branches against bright sky).
   - **JPEG recompression and small tampered regions reduce reliability**; Chen et al. show FRR increases substantially going from RAW→JPEG90→JPEG75, and localization accuracy for very small (<64×64) tampered regions is poor without careful block-size tuning (see the Korus & Huang multi-scale approach for mitigation).
   - **Cross-domain noise baselines differ** (confirmed indirectly by the Policy-Gradient-Driven-Noise-Mask paper's medical-imaging findings) — a single fixed "expected noise level" threshold will not generalize across camera models/ISO settings/compression levels; the block-score-vs-local-neighborhood-median comparison (rather than a fixed absolute threshold) is more robust than a fixed global constant.
   - **Blind/no-reference mode (no source camera) is strictly weaker** than reference-based PRNU matching; for this engine (single suspect image, no camera database) prefer the self-consistency / local-block-comparison approach (regions compared to each other within the same image) over absolute camera-fingerprint matching, reserving reference-based PRNU for cases where a candidate source camera or multiple images from the same device are available.
