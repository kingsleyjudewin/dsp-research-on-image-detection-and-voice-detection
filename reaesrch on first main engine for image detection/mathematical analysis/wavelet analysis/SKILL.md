# Wavelet-Domain Forgery Detection

## Purpose
Wavelet decomposition gives a multi-resolution, spatially-localized split of an image into low-frequency (approximation) and high-frequency (horizontal/vertical/diagonal detail) subbands at each decomposition level. Camera sensor noise, in-camera processing (demosaicing, denoising, sharpening), and lossy compression each leave characteristic, statistically-predictable fingerprints in the high-frequency detail subbands — clean natural-image wavelet coefficients follow a sharply-peaked Laplacian/generalized-Gaussian distribution, while processing (recompression, splicing, resampling, denoising) perturbs this distribution or introduces block-boundary and quantization artifacts. Because wavelet analysis is local in both space and frequency, it lets a forensic engine (a) extract a noise-residual image (the "camera fingerprint" or PRNU-adjacent signal) by discarding the low-frequency image content and keeping the fine-scale residual, and (b) localize *where* an anomaly occurs, not just detect that one exists — critical for tamper/splice localization rather than whole-image binary classification. This is the mathematical basis for using wavelet-domain statistics as one of the fusion-layer forensic features in this engine.

## Techniques found in the literature

**1. Detection of Copy-Move Forgery Using Wavelet Decomposition**
Source: `kashyap2013.pdf` (Kashyap & Joshi, IEEE 2013)
Most directly relevant paper. Pipeline: (1) single-level Haar DWT of the image, keeping only the coarse (LL/approximation) subband to cut data volume while preserving structure; (2) tile the coarse subband into overlapping R×R blocks; (3) represent each block by 24 blur-moment invariants (functions of central image moments, invariant to convolution with an unknown point-spread function, i.e. robust to blur/noise/JPEG artifacts introduced by the tampering process) — RGB images produce a 72-length vector; (4) PCA to reduce feature dimensionality; (5) block-similarity search via Euclidean distance with a similarity threshold T and a minimum spatial-distance threshold D so that trivially near-identical neighboring blocks (smooth regions) aren't flagged; (6) 16-neighborhood consistency check on candidate block pairs to confirm a genuinely duplicated region rather than an isolated coincidental match; (7) build a binary "duplicated region map." Reported 86.67% detection accuracy on a blind mix of 15 authentic/forged JPEG images, with substantially lower runtime than the prior blur-moment method it improves on (Mahdian & Saic 2007) because wavelet pre-decomposition shrinks the search space before block matching. Tradeoff: analysis restricted to un-rotated/unscaled copy-move; robustness to scaled/rotated duplicated regions is left as future work.

**2. Wavelet-Based Image Compression Anti-Forensics**
Source: `stamm2010.pdf` (Stamm & Liu, ICIP 2010)
Directly relevant as the *detection-side* mechanism this paper attacks: wavelet-based compressors (SPIHT, EZW, JPEG2000) leave quantization/bitstream-truncation artifacts in the wavelet coefficient histograms of each subband — coefficients cluster at discrete quantization levels instead of following a smooth Laplacian distribution, which is exactly the signal a forensic classifier uses to detect "this image was wavelet-compressed" (histogram peaks at quantized values vs. Laplace-shaped uncompressed histogram, illustrated in Fig. 1/3 of the paper). The paper's stated forensic detector (Lin et al. 2009, cited as [2]) achieves 99.6% classification accuracy on SPIHT-compressed vs. never-compressed images using exactly this histogram-shape statistic. The paper itself is an anti-forensic countermeasure (adds "anti-forensic dither" to restore a Laplacian-looking histogram and fool the detector 100% of the time in their tests) — useful to know as a known limitation/adversarial threat model for any wavelet-histogram-based compression-history detector built into this engine.

**3. A Review of Wavelet Transform based Techniques for Denoising Latent Fingerprint Images**
Source: `mgaga2019.pdf` (Mgaga, Khanyile & Tapamo, 2019)
Marginally relevant to forgery per se (it's a biometric denoising survey) but highly useful as a practical recipe for computing wavelet noise-residuals/thresholds, which this engine can reuse for its own residual-extraction stage. Covers: (a) standard 2D DWT subband decomposition (LL/LH/HL/HH per level); (b) hard vs. soft thresholding rules; (c) threshold-value computation methods — VisuShrink/universal threshold T = σ√(2 log N), BayesShrink T = σ²_noise/σ_signal, and an adaptive per-subband/per-level threshold; (d) noise standard deviation estimation from the diagonal (HH) detail subband via the robust median estimator σ = median(|HH|)/0.6745 — this is the standard practical way to estimate per-image noise level directly from wavelet coefficients without a reference clean image. Recommends stationary wavelet transform (SWT, translation-invariant, no downsampling) over conventional DWT, and a weighted-median noise estimator over the plain median, for better residual quality. Reports wavelet filter choice matters (Haar = cheap/simple; db2/sym4 = smoother, better for speckle noise) and decomposition level 2 was consistently near-optimal in the reviewed studies.

**4. Image Denoising by Wavelet Transform Based on New Threshold**
Source: `Image-Denoising-by-Wavelet-Transform-Based-on-New-Threshold.pdf` (Zhu & Wang, 2021)
Marginally relevant — general-purpose denoising background technique, not forgery-specific, but the thresholding formula is directly reusable in the residual-extraction stage. Proposes a piecewise "streamlined wavelet shrinkage" function that behaves like hard thresholding above λ1, blends toward zero smoothly (rather than hard-cutting) between λ2 and λ1, and zeroes out below λ2, with λ1 = σ√(2 ln N + 2 ln ln N) and λ2 = ασ√(2 ln N + 2 ln ln N) (a scaled universal threshold), tested with a 3-level sym4 decomposition. Reported improvement over plain hard/soft thresholding: SNR 29.67 dB vs. 28.35/28.58 dB, MSE 0.097 vs. 0.121/0.112. Useful mainly as a smoother alternative thresholding function if the engine's residual extraction needs to reduce oscillation/Gibbs artifacts from hard thresholding.

**5. Multi-stage image denoising with the wavelet transform**
Source: `Multi-stage-image-denoising-with-the-wavelet-transform.pdf` (Tian et al., Pattern Recognition 2023)
Marginally relevant — this is a deep-learning (CNN) denoiser, not a forensic technique, included here only as background. Combines a dynamic convolutional block, two cascaded "wavelet transform + enhancement" blocks (which fuse the DWT's frequency-domain features with CNN-learned structural features), and a residual block, trained end-to-end (outperforming DnCNN/ADNet baselines on standard denoising benchmarks). Relevance to this engine is limited to the general idea that wavelet subbands can be fed as auxiliary features into a learned model rather than used only with classical thresholding — worth keeping in mind if the fusion layer later incorporates a learned component, but no forensic/forgery mechanism here.

**6. Study of Wavelet Transform-based Image Fusion Methods**
Source: `cheng2021.pdf` (Cheng et al., J. Phys. Conf. Ser. 2021)
Marginally relevant — image fusion (combining two source images), not forgery detection. Useful only for the standard multi-resolution decomposition/fusion-rule vocabulary it reiterates: decompose each source image via DWT into low-frequency (energy/approximation) and high-frequency (detail) components, fuse low-frequency via weighted averaging and high-frequency via absolute-maximum selection, then invert. Confirms wavelet-based fusion outperforms Laplacian-pyramid fusion on standard objective metrics (entropy, spatial frequency, average gradient) in their tests. No forensic content; skip for implementation purposes beyond terminology.

**7. Image Compression Using Hybrid Radon Transform with Discrete Wavelet Transform Technique**
Source: `Image-Compression-Using-Hybrid-Radon-Transform-with-Discrete-Wavelet-Transform-Technique.pdf` (Nanmaran et al., Springer LNNS 2024)
Marginally relevant — a compression technique (Radon transform for line-integral feature extraction, then DWT to encode those features compactly), evaluated on PSNR (28.73 dB), SSIM (89.41%), and Compression Ratio (88.33%), aimed at medical imaging. No forgery-detection content in the excerpted sections. Relevant to this engine only tangentially: reinforces that DWT coefficient statistics are a standard compact representation, and that PSNR/SSIM are the expected quality metrics to report if this engine's wavelet stage also needs a compression or recompression-quality-estimation submodule.

## Recommended approach for this engine

**Transform and decomposition level.** Use a 2D discrete wavelet transform (DWT) as the default per-block/per-image decomposition; prefer the **stationary wavelet transform (SWT/undecimated DWT)** over decimated DWT for the noise-residual extraction step specifically, since SWT is translation-invariant and avoids the down/upsampling artifacts that make DWT-based residuals inconsistent between neighboring pixel positions — a real problem when later doing tamper *localization* rather than whole-image classification. Wavelet family: **Daubechies (db2/db4) or Symlets (sym4)** for the residual/statistics path (smoother filters correlate better with sensor/processing noise than Haar, per `mgaga2019.pdf`'s comparison), but keep **Haar** available as a cheap first-pass option for block-matching/copy-move search where speed matters more than fine texture fidelity (as in `kashyap2013.pdf`). Decomposition level: **2–3 levels** is the practically-validated sweet spot across the reviewed papers (level 2 recurs as optimal in `mgaga2019.pdf`'s summary table; `Image-Denoising...New-Threshold.pdf` used 3-level sym4). Deeper decomposition risks distorting/over-smoothing the residual signal.

**Extracting the forensic residual / multi-resolution feature.**
1. Decompose the image (or fixed-size overlapping blocks, e.g. 16×16 or 32×32, for localization) via SWT/DWT to 2–3 levels, producing LL (approximation) and LH/HL/HH (detail) subbands per level.
2. Estimate the per-subband noise level directly from the diagonal HH detail coefficients using the robust median estimator: σ = median(|HH|) / 0.6745 (standard in `mgaga2019.pdf`, also used in `stamm2010.pdf`'s underlying model). This gives a per-region noise-level statistic without needing a reference "clean" image.
3. Apply thresholding to isolate the residual: either the classical universal/VisuShrink threshold T = σ√(2 log N), or the smoother piecewise shrinkage function from `Image-Denoising...New-Threshold.pdf` (λ1/λ2 with a blended middle region) if hard-threshold Gibbs artifacts are a concern for the fusion layer's downstream feature stability. The **denoising residual** (original coefficients minus thresholded/denoised coefficients) is the forensic signal of interest — analogous to SRM/PRNU-style high-frequency noise residuals used elsewhere in image forensics, but computed via wavelet subbands instead of fixed high-pass filters.
4. For compression-history / recompression detection specifically, additionally fit the HH-subband coefficient histogram (per level) to the Laplacian model P(X=x) = (λ/2)e^(−λ|x|) via weighted least squares (formulas in `stamm2010.pdf` §3.1–3.2) and flag deviation from the expected smooth peak (clustering at discrete quantization values) as evidence of prior wavelet-based lossy compression. Be aware this specific detector is defeatable by anti-forensic dithering (same paper) — do not rely on it as a sole signal; combine with other fusion-layer cues.

**Feeding into noise-pattern / PRNU-based detection and tamper scoring.**
- Treat the extracted wavelet noise-residual as one of the inputs to the engine's noise-pattern comparison stage (alongside any PRNU/camera-fingerprint correlation module elsewhere in the pipeline): compute local noise-level statistics (σ estimates per block, per subband/level) across the image and flag blocks whose noise statistics deviate significantly from the image-wide (or expected-camera) baseline — inconsistent local noise level is a classic splicing/tamper indicator, and the wavelet subband decomposition gives this at multiple spatial scales for free.
- For copy-move specifically, implement the `kashyap2013.pdf` pipeline directly: DWT → coarse-subband block tiling → blur-moment-invariant feature vector per block → PCA → block similarity search with a similarity threshold T and minimum-distance threshold D → 16-neighbor consistency check → duplicated-region map. This produces a spatial tamper-localization map, not just a scalar score.
- **Turning this into a fusion-layer tampering score:** aggregate per-block anomaly signals (noise-statistic deviation z-score, copy-move block-match density, compression-histogram deviation) into a normalized [0,1] score per region — e.g. score = max or weighted-sum of (a) normalized |σ_block − σ_image_baseline|, (b) fraction of blocks in a duplicated-region map, (c) compression-histogram Laplacian-fit residual — then pass both the scalar score and the spatial map to the fusion layer so it can combine with other modality outputs (audio/video forgery scores) and still support visual tamper localization in the final report.

**Known limitations.**
- Wavelet-histogram compression detectors are provably defeatable by dedicated anti-forensic dithering (`stamm2010.pdf`) — treat as a weak/corroborating signal, not authoritative.
- Copy-move detection via blur-moment invariants on wavelet-decomposed blocks (`kashyap2013.pdf`) does not handle scaled or rotated duplicated regions and tops out around 86.67% accuracy in the source study — expect false negatives on geometrically-transformed copy-move forgeries; consider supplementing with a rotation/scale-invariant keypoint method if that threat is in scope.
- DWT-based residuals are shift-variant (blocking/aliasing artifacts at decimation boundaries); use SWT for any step requiring precise pixel-level localization.
- Noise-level estimation via median(|HH|)/0.6745 assumes additive, roughly Gaussian noise; it degrades under signal-dependent noise (e.g. speckle from certain sensors) — `mgaga2019.pdf` notes speckle-specific techniques (NAWT) may be needed for those cases.
- None of the 7 papers reviewed here directly implement a full wavelet-domain PRNU/camera-fingerprint pipeline; the PRNU linkage above is a design recommendation synthesized from the denoising-residual literature, not something validated end-to-end in any single source paper — validate empirically before treating it as a primary detector.
