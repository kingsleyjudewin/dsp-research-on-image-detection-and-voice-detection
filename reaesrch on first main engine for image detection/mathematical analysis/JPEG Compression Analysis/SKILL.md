# JPEG Compression Artifact Forgery Detection

## Purpose

JPEG is a lossy, block-based (8x8 DCT) codec, and every time a JPEG is decoded, edited, and re-saved, it leaves a statistical fingerprint of that second compression in the DCT coefficient distributions and in the 8x8 block grid. When an attacker splices a region from one image into another and re-saves the composite as JPEG, the pasted region and the background typically carry *different* compression histories: the background has been JPEG-compressed twice (once originally, once again on save), while the pasted region may be single-compressed (if it came from an uncompressed/PNG source), double-compressed with different quantization steps (if it came from a differently-compressed JPEG), or double-compressed with a block grid that is spatially shifted relative to the host image's grid (if the paste was not pixel-aligned to 8x8 boundaries). These inconsistencies — double/multiple quantization periodicity in DCT histograms, mismatched quantization tables, and misaligned block grids — are invisible to the eye but statistically detectable, making JPEG compression-artifact analysis one of the most reliable low-level signals for localizing splicing and re-saving forgeries, independent of scene content.

## Techniques found in the literature

### 1. "JPEG Error Analysis and Its Applications to Digital Image Forensics"
- **Filename**: `luo2010.pdf`
- **Authors**: Weiqi Luo, Jiwu Huang, Guoping Qiu — IEEE TIFS, Vol. 5, No. 3, 2010
- **Technique**: Theoretically models the three error sources in JPEG compression/recompression — quantization error, rounding error, and truncation error — as an additive noise model on DCT coefficients: `d2 = d1' + ε` where `ε` is approximately Gaussian (mean 0, variance 1/12) due to rounding. From this model they derive three tools: (a) a 1-D feature `s = ∫_R2 p_ac(y)dy / ∫_R1 p_ac(y)dy` (ratio of AC coefficient histogram mass in regions R1=(-1,1) vs R2=(-2,-1)∪(1,2)) that separates JPEG-compressed from never-compressed bitmaps; (b) quantization step estimation per frequency by finding the histogram bin of `[d2]` (rounded dequantized coefficients) with maximum count; (c) quantization table detection by recompressing a candidate image with all 100 standard QF tables and picking the table whose recompressed image `J2` is pixel-identical (via similarity measure `R(J1,J2) = |E|/MN`) to the image under test most often.
- **Results/tradeoffs**: Outperforms Fridrich's method and Fan's blocking-artifact method, especially on small images (down to 8x8 blocks) and high QFs (up to 98) where existing methods fail; ~95-98% JPEG-identification accuracy even at 8x8 block size. Weaker at very low QF (QF=50) for high-frequency quantization steps because those coefficients are quantized to zero, destroying the statistic. Only tested on grayscale; extension to color is claimed straightforward but not demonstrated.

### 2. "Detecting Double Compressed JPEG Images"
- **Filename**: `mahdian2009.pdf`
- **Authors**: Babak Mahdian, Stanislav Saic — Institute of Information Theory and Automation of the ASCR
- **Technique**: Builds on Popescu & Farid's DQ (double-quantization) effect: after double JPEG compression with primary/secondary quantization steps `Q_α`, `Q_β`, the DCT coefficient histograms develop periodic peaks/zeros detectable via the Fourier transform of the zero-mean histogram. They compute FFT magnitudes `|H_i|` of histograms at 10 low-frequency DCT positions ((0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(3,0),(0,1),(1,1),(2,1),(0,2),(1,2),(0,3)), luminance channel only. Key contribution: instead of Popescu/Farid's complex 2-parameter generalized-Laplace curve fit, they use a simple local-minimum subtraction (`|H̃_i|(f) = |H_i|(f) - M_i(f)`, minimum over a neighborhood window) to strip the decaying trend and expose local peaks cheaply. Only AC components are used (10 low frequencies); classification is per-quantization-step SVM (Gaussian kernel) trained on peak-position feature vectors.
- **Results/tradeoffs**: Significantly fewer false positives than the Popescu/Farid baseline on natural images with "non-perfect" (non-Gaussian/non-Laplacian) histograms and on scanned/untextured images. Detection accuracy is near-perfect when secondary QF > primary QF (shorter histogram support, larger period) but drops sharply, sometimes to ~0%, when primary and secondary quantization steps share an integer ratio (`Q_β/Q_α ∈ Z`), since no DQ artifact is introduced in that case — a fundamental blind spot shared by all DQ-histogram methods.

### 3. "Double JPEG compression forensics based on a convolutional neural network"
- **Filename**: `s13635-016-0047-y.pdf`
- **Authors**: Qing Wang, Rong Zhang — EURASIP Journal on Information Security, 2016:23
- **Technique**: Converts the DQ-histogram detection problem into a learned classification problem. For each 8x8-aligned block, extracts a 9x11 hand-crafted feature: for each of the 2nd-10th AC frequencies (zigzag order), the histogram value at bins {-5,...,5} around the peak. Feeds this into a small 1-D CNN (2 conv+pool blocks, kernel 3x1, 100 filters, 3 fully-connected layers, softmax) trained to classify each block as singly- or doubly-compressed. Localization is obtained by sliding overlapping WxW blocks (stride 8) across the image and building a block posterior probability map (BPPM) from the softmax output.
- **Results/tradeoffs**: Outperforms Bayesian-approach (DQ likelihood) and SVM/first-digit-feature baselines, especially for small blocks (down to 64x64) and when QF2 < QF1 — the case classically hardest for DQ-histogram methods. AUC ~0.86-0.96 depending on QF pair. Still constructs a separate classifier per QF2 (needs QF2 known/enumerable) and does not solve the case QF1=QF2 explicitly; only Y-channel AC coefficients are used, and full end-to-end pixel-domain learning is not attempted (input is still a hand-crafted histogram feature, not raw pixels).

### 4. "Aligned and Non-Aligned Double JPEG Detection Using Convolutional Neural Networks"
- **Filename**: `barni2017.pdf`
- **Authors**: M. Barni, L. Bondi, N. Bonettini, P. Bestagini, A. Costanzo, M. Maggini, B. Tondi, S. Tubaro — Journal of Visual Communication and Image Representation, 2017
- **Technique**: Directly addresses the two double-JPEG scenarios separately: Aligned DJPEG (A-DJPEG, second compression grid coincides with the first) and Non-Aligned DJPEG (NA-DJPEG, grid is spatially shifted). Proposes and compares three CNN variants: `C_hist` (CNN over concatenated DCT-coefficient histograms, similar in spirit to Wang & Zhang but with correlation across histograms exploited via CNN layers), `C_pix` (CNN fed raw pixel values, fully self-learned features), and `C_noise` (CNN fed high-pass/noise-residual images, i.e., a pre-processing filtering step to expose compression artifacts before the conv layers, following the median-filter-forensics CNN precedent). All work on small 64x64 patches for localization.
- **Results/tradeoffs**: `C_hist` (hand-crafted DCT histogram features) wins for A-DJPEG but fails on NA-DJPEG because DCT-domain periodicity is destroyed by grid misalignment. `C_noise` (self-learned features on noise residuals) is the most robust overall: it outperforms state-of-the-art (including [18] Bianchi & Piva periodicity-map method) on NA-DJPEG in every tested scenario, including the hard case QF1 > QF2 and even QF1 = QF2, and it generalizes reasonably to QF pairs not seen during training (Table 4: accuracy 0.6-0.97 depending on how far test QFs are from training QFs, degrading toward chance as QF gap widens or block size shrinks). A single detector (`C_pix`/`C_noise`) can handle both A- and NA-DJPEG without knowing in advance which scenario applies, and — because it works directly on pixels — it also works on images distributed as bitmap/PNG (an antiforensic evasion of methods that need JPEG bitstream access). Weak spot: when QF2 is very high (e.g. 95) and QF1 > 95, accuracy caps around 66% because the second compression barely disturbs already-weak traces from the first.

## Recommended approach for this engine

**Primary detector — self-learned CNN on noise residuals (`C_noise`-style), operating on sliding 64x64 patches.**
1. Pre-process each candidate patch with a high-pass/noise-residual filter (as in Barni et al. and the median-filtering-forensics precedent) before feeding it to a small CNN (2-3 conv/pool blocks is sufficient at this patch size). This single architecture handles both aligned and non-aligned double compression without needing to know in advance whether tampering paste-boundaries fall on 8x8 grid lines — a real requirement since attackers rarely align pastes to the block grid.
2. Slide 64x64 (or smaller, down to 8x8 per Luo et al. if resolution is very small) overlapping blocks with stride 8-16px across the full image; run the classifier per block to get a per-block "double-compressed" probability, forming a block posterior probability map (BPPM/heatmap) directly usable by the fusion layer as one modality's tampering-likelihood map. Splice regions typically appear as a spatially compact blob of anomalous (single-compression, or differently-quantized) blocks against a doubly-compressed background, or vice versa.
3. **Quantization-table / QF fingerprinting as a secondary, complementary signal**: implement Luo et al.'s lightweight quantization-step estimator (histogram-bin-of-max-count on rounded dequantized coefficients) and their recompression-matching quantization-table detector. Use this to (a) flag whether the whole image is even a plausible single-JPEG-history image (a strong prior for "unmanipulated" or "single resave"), and (b) as an auxiliary feature per region — regions whose locally-estimated quantization table disagrees with the globally dominant table are suspicious even without a full DQ-CNN pass. This is cheap (closed-form, no training) and works as a fast first-pass filter before invoking the CNN.
4. **DQ-histogram / Fourier-periodicity check** (Mahdian & Saic style) as a fallback/ensemble member for the aligned case specifically, since it needs no training data and is robust to domain shift in a way CNNs are not; combine its output with the CNN score (e.g., simple averaging or a small random-forest fusor over {C_hist output, C_noise output}, as Barni et al. do for the joint A-/NA- case, gaining ~2% accuracy).

**Turning this into a tampering score for fusion:**
- Aggregate the block-level probability map into (a) a continuous heatmap (resize/interpolate BPPM to full image resolution, feed to the module fusion layer as one channel), and (b) a single scalar tampering score per image, e.g., the fraction of blocks whose probability exceeds a threshold, or the max contiguous-blob size/confidence — useful when the fusion layer wants a compact per-image feature alongside heatmaps from other DSP modules (ELA, noise, PRNU, etc.).
- Calibrate thresholds separately per estimated "background" QF, since detection accuracy is strongly QF-pair-dependent (all four papers show this); if the background JPEG's QF can be estimated (Luo et al.'s method), use it to pick the right operating threshold/model variant, or ensure the training set spans the full QF range likely encountered.

**Known limitations to document/handle upstream in the engine:**
- **Single-compression images**: An image compressed only once carries no DQ artifact at all — this whole module category is blind to single-generation forgeries (e.g., manipulation done entirely in the RAW/uncompressed domain before the one and only JPEG save, or splicing where both source and host share identical quantization tables so `Q_β/Q_α` is integer and no periodic artifact appears — a specific, systematic blind spot noted by Mahdian & Saic).
- **Non-JPEG sources**: If the pasted region originated from a PNG/BMP/other lossless source and the whole composite is saved once as JPEG, that region is single-compressed while the background is double-compressed — this is actually the *easiest* case for these methods to catch. But if the whole composite pipeline never touches JPEG at all (pure PNG workflow), this module category yields no signal and must be excluded from the fusion vote (weight ~0) rather than reported as "clean."
- **Low resolution / small tampered regions**: All methods degrade as patch size shrinks below ~64x64 (and severely below 32x32), and as QF gets very low (QF≈50, where high-frequency coefficients are all quantized to zero, destroying the statistics needed by both DQ-histogram and quantization-step methods). For very small suspected regions, prefer the CNN-based methods (Barni et al., Wang & Zhang) over classical DQ-Fourier (Mahdian & Saic), since the CNNs were explicitly validated down to 8x8-64x64 blocks; the classical FFT-periodicity method needs more samples for a stable histogram and is not reliable below ~256x256 blocks.
- **QF1 > QF2 with QF2 near the ceiling (≈95-100)**: this remains an unsolved hard case across all four papers — flag it as a known failure mode with capped confidence (~50-66%) rather than trusting a high score.
- **Training/domain generalization**: CNN-based detectors need training data spanning the QF pairs, image sources, and camera pipelines expected in production; accuracy drops measurably (though gracefully with `C_noise`) when test QF pairs diverge from training QF pairs.
