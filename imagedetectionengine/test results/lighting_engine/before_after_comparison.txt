PART 2 - GROUND TRUTH: DOES THE STATISTIC SEE LIGHTING AT ALL?
A Lambertian scene of five spheres on a plane, rendered from ONE fixed
geometry. In the consistent version every sphere is lit from direction L1. In
each inconsistent version exactly one sphere is lit from a different direction,
with its geometry, albedo, position and size untouched - so the ONLY difference
between the images is the illumination of one object, which is precisely the
property this engine is named for. Verified local: the images differ only
inside that sphere's bounding box, 3.2-6.0% of the frame, with a maximum pixel
delta of 178-192 out of 255. Identical texture is added to every variant so the
engine runs non-degenerate.

  case                     raw_score   delta vs consistent
  ---------------------------------------------------------
  consistent                 21.3564   +0.0000
  inconsistent_mild          21.3564   +0.0000
  inconsistent_two           21.3564   +0.0000
  inconsistent_gross         21.3564   +0.0000

  A sphere re-lit from BELOW instead of from the upper left changes the score
  by EXACTLY ZERO, to four decimal places. The reason is structural: raw_score
  is the maximum over the whole frame, that maximum lives on some silhouette
  elsewhere in the image, and re-lighting the interior of one object does not
  touch it.

  Meanwhile, transformations that change NO lighting whatsoever:

  transform                raw_score   delta vs original
  ---------------------------------------------------------
  original                   21.3564   +0.0000
  unsharp                    18.5762   -2.7802
  jpeg_q90                   20.6468   -0.7096
  crop 90%                   21.1540   -0.2024
  ONE PIXEL changed          22.0666   +0.7101
  upscale 1.5x               24.9825   +3.6261
  downscale 0.75x            30.4138   +9.0574
  gaussian blur 3x3          32.0010   +10.6446
  downscale 0.5x             34.4477   +13.0913
  jpeg quality 60            36.3184   +14.9620

  Changing a SINGLE PIXEL moves the score by 0.7101 - infinitely more than any
  genuine lighting inconsistency does, because that is 0.0000. Re-saving the
  same picture at JPEG quality 60 moves it by 14.96.

PART 3 - NO REDUCTION OF THIS MAP FIXES IT
Every candidate replacement for max() was measured on the same ground truth.
Sensitivity to a real lighting inconsistency, against sensitivity to the
nuisance transforms above:

  statistic       lighting     nuisance    nuisance/lighting
  ------------------------------------------------------------
  max/median      0.000000        14.96    1.5e13
  p99.99/median   0.134190        14.53      108.3
  p99.9/median    0.887615        13.20       14.9
  p99/median      0.004327         5.27     1217.2
  mean/median     0.000128         0.204    1593.4
  std/median      0.003716         1.047     281.8

  The best of them is still 13.8x more sensitive to nuisance than to lighting.
  This is not a tuning problem. The gradient-magnitude map does not carry the
  information, so no way of reducing it can recover the information.

PART 4 - WHAT THE SCORE ACTUALLY RESTS ON
  Pixels within 1% of the maximum, per supplied image: 1, 1, 172, 2, 3, 1.
  On five of the six, raw_score is decided by ONE pixel out of one to two
  million. On the sixth ("fake .jpeg") the 172 tied pixels were all on the
  array's own boundary, where numpy.gradient switches to a one-sided
  difference - the engine was measuring the edge of the array, not the picture.
  Gradient distributions run p50 = 0.5-1.0 against a max of 83-196, so the
  statistic is a ratio of the most extreme sample in the frame to the middle
  of a distribution it sits three orders of magnitude above.

HONEST RESIDUAL DEFECTS
  - The engine now produces NO probability at all by default. It is, for
    fusion purposes, switched off unless an orchestrator supplies at least 30
    known-authentic reference ratios. Verified that this path works: with 40
    reference ratios the same image returns probability 0.4250 at
    is_reliable=True. But even then the underlying feature is still an
    edge-strength statistic with zero measured sensitivity to lighting, so the
    calibrated route buys a well-defined number, not a valid one.
  - raw_score remains dominated by a single pixel. This was not changed,
    because the SKILL prints max_grad = max(gradient_mag(:)) verbatim and no
    alternative reduction was measured to help.
  - No spatial localisation. flagged_regions is always None, correctly: the
    SKILL gives no procedure for turning the gradient map into suspect regions.
  - The median gradient of an ordinary photograph is 0.5-1.0, which sits close
    to the 0.01 degeneracy floor. A slightly flatter image tips into the
    degenerate branch, so the engine's operating regime is narrower than it
    looks.
